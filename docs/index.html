The scene is the primitive.

Coordination infrastructure for communities — starting as a Luma you can never be held hostage by, growing into the trust fabric that lets scenes self-organize, and eventually steward real resources.

“Scenius stands for the intelligence and the intuition of a whole cultural scene. It is the communal form of the concept of the genius.” — Brian Eno

The kernel

Every event platform treats the event as the atom and the host as the customer. We invert it: the atom is the scene — a living cultural or geographic community whose calendar, membership, trust, and resources are facets of one thing.

Everything below follows from that single inversion.

01The Wedge

A Luma replacement that’s deliberately boring

Events, RSVPs, capacity & waitlists with real arbitration, reminders, iCal, a Luma importer, scene newsletters. Nobody adopts a trust graph — they adopt a better event tool. The boringness is load-bearing.

The honest pitch: 0% fees (payments host-owned, money never touches us), your community is yours — not CRM rows in someone’s SaaS — and you can leave with everything. Which, paradoxically, is the strongest reason to stay. RegenHub-off-Luma is the proof case.

Substantially built & tested
02The Multiplier

Agent-native, by architecture

Every capability ships three ways — web UI, HTTP API, MCP — by design, not as a bolt-on. The bet: discovery is about to stop being “scroll a feed” and become “ask your agent.” “What’s happening in Boulder this weekend that people I trust are going to?” is an MCP query, not a browsing session.

Platforms that are agent-legible win ambient discovery by default. Platforms that aren’t become invisible to the interface people increasingly actually use.

MCP read surface live
03The Flywheel

Scenes, not algorithms

For attendees: calendars curated by humans embedded in the community, plus a city-level feed across scenes. For hosts: post once, appear everywhere — every scene whose builders pick your event up, every interoperable app, every agent.

Better discovery → more attendees → more hosts → richer calendars. The crucial difference from Luma’s flywheel: ours is open — which is the next rung.

04The Inversion

Built on AT Protocol — we own behavior, never the canon

Data lives in users’ own repos under shared lexicons; scenius is an AppView on top. Three consequences, plainly: free interop — an event here shows up in Smoke Signal, Dandelion, OpenMeet, and vice versa; the network effect accrues to the ecosystem, not to us. Credible exit — every prior community platform eventually held its communities hostage; we can’t, even if we wanted to, and that’s a feature you can pitch. A commons others build on — per-scene apps, alternate views, venue integrations, all over the same records.

Growth doesn’t require us winning. It requires the commons growing.

05The Heart

Trust that compounds into self-organization

The primitives: membership with roles, curation as an additive gate (builders pull events onto the scene calendar), and attestation — peer vouching, the web-of-trust substrate.

Governance is a gradient: scenes start administered (a founder + a few builders — which is how legitimacy actually bootstraps), and can ratchet toward emergent, attestation-conferred stewardship — a field flip, not a migration. Self-organizing ≠ leaderless; it means distributed, legitimate, transferable stewardship — with forking as the ultimate backstop, because members own their own records and can walk with the graph.

The design signature

The same move, three times

Meet people where they are. Ratchet toward sovereignty. Never force it.

Governance
administered → emergent

A scene starts founder-led and ratchets toward peer-conferred stewardship as trust thickens.

Identity
email → DID

People join a scene’s list with just an email; later, the same history bridges into a sovereign, portable identity.

Curation
open → featured

Anyone can post; scenes and editors shape what’s surfaced. Curated reads over permissionless writes.

06The Horizon

Communities that can direct resources

Once a scene has a legible, portable trust graph plus a track record of real coordination — events that actually happened, who showed up, who hosted, who vouched — it becomes a credible unit for stewarding resources: scene treasuries first (0% passthrough), then participatory funding where the trust fabric gates eligibility.

Communities that can demonstrate their own coherence can absorb funding effectively — instead of funding flowing to whoever writes the best grant.

The guardrail we never blur: belonging ≠ allocation weight. Attestation answers “do you belong?” — never directly “who gets paid?” — or the trust graph becomes a target and the heart corrupts.

Sustainability

Charge for what costs us — never for what belongs to them.

Never a ticket rake: 0% on tickets, forever. Open data and open code mean anyone could undercut a rake using our own commons — the protocol forces an honest model. Revenue is service, not extraction: venue & org tooling, newsletter volume, an AI events-coordinator for hosts, managed city instances.

Attendees ride free — and so do their agents. API & MCP reads are cheap structured queries, effectively free to serve, so they stay open; paid tiers begin only at sustained commercial scale. We run the legible substrate — you bring your own intelligence. Charge for convenience, never for connection.
